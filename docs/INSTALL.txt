SSH OS: putting it on a Macintosh SE
====================================

What you need

  - A Macintosh SE with the FDHD ROM (the one with the 1.4 MB "SuperDrive";
    it is the only ROM SSH OS has been tried with) and 4 MB of memory.
  - A BlueSCSI v2 with the Pico W, the WiFi model, inside or outside the SE.
  - An SD card for it. Use a card of its own: SSH OS takes the machine over
    from the first moment, and your Mac OS card stays as it is.

There is nothing to install on the SE and nothing to build. The files in
this folder are the whole system.

Steps

  1. Format the SD card as FAT32 or exFAT (MS-DOS FAT in Disk Utility).

  2. Copy these three files to the top of the card, names unchanged:

       HD0_512 SSH OS boot.hda    SSH OS itself; the SE starts from this
       HD1_512 SSH OS.hda         where SSH OS keeps what it learns: your
                                  WiFi, the servers you connect to, its key
       NE4.hda                    an empty file; it switches on the
                                  BlueSCSI's network adapter

  3. Eject the card, put it in the BlueSCSI, switch the SE on. The start-up
     picture shows for a few seconds, then the home screen.

  4. Join your WiFi: click the fan at the right of the bar (or press W),
     click your network, type its password, Return. It is remembered.

  5. Click the SSH icon, then New connection, and type user@server (add
     :port if it is not 22). Connect.

Good to know

  - Connecting takes about a minute: the 68000 does all of SSH's
    arithmetic itself. The bar on the screen shows how far it is, and
    Cancel (or Esc) stops it.
  - The first time, SSH OS shows the server's fingerprint and asks whether
    to trust it; after your password it asks whether to log in without one
    from then on. Both answers are kept on the card.
  - The clock is the SE's own. If its battery is flat the time is wrong;
    nothing else minds.
  - To go back to Mac OS, swap the cards. SSH OS writes nothing to any
    other disk.
  - Nothing here is Apple's: the loader and the system are SSH OS's own
    code, under the MIT licence (see LICENSE).

If it does not start

  - A blinking question mark: the SE found no disk to start from. Check
    that the file names are exactly as above and that the card is FAT32 or
    exFAT; the BlueSCSI writes what it found to log.txt on the card.
  - "offline" in the bar: the network adapter is missing (is NE4.hda on the
    card? is this a Pico W?) or the WiFi is not joined yet (step 4).

More, and the source: https://github.com/naruse/SSH-OS
